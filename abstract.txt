Self-play reinforcement learning typically studies how agents improve within a fixed environment. This research note proposes extending that paradigm by treating the environment itself as a variable in the learning process. The proposed framework constructs families of related computational environments whose rules can be systematically varied, allowing agents to train through self-play across both familiar and previously unseen environments. Using a game-like environment as an initial testbed, the study would investigate whether exposure to structured environmental variation produces strategies that generalize beyond the specific environments encountered during training. The framework also creates an opportunity to study how environmental structure influences learning dynamics, strategy diversity, and transfer. Rather than presenting a completed empirical study, this note outlines the research question, experimental framework, and initial hypotheses that motivate future implementation and experimentation.
